Two kinds of friction

The tallies above count every obstacle as “friction”. The dev team and the instructional design team need different things from that number, so the report now splits it in two. The original verdicts are unchanged; this is a second reading of the same evidence.

Unintended UI/UX friction

Target: 0 · Owner: dev team · Fix it

An obstacle the software or layout puts in the way by accident. Removing it takes nothing away from the skill being practiced.

  • The Job Card covers the button to press
  • Misleading button labels (“Clock out for today” signs you out)
  • Story dates or schedules that contradict each other
  • Broken validation: a check that rejects honest beginner English or accepts a wrong answer
  • Names, roles or routes that slip; tools that behave unlike the real app

Intentional pedagogical friction

Target: keep and refine · Owner: instructional design

A deliberate challenge that builds the skill: find the right shift time, write a clear workplace email, check the payroll math. It should stay hard enough to need thinking, and be fair and supportive.

Fair and supportive means:

  • The facts needed are on screen or one app away
  • The prompt is at the learner’s English level
  • The answer is not shown before the learner tries
  • A wrong try gets a specific, kind correction

How to sort an obstacle

Ask: if this obstacle disappeared, would the learner practice less of the skill? No → unintended; fix it. Yes → intentional; keep it and make it fair.

Two edge cases. A broken check on a real learning task counts as unintended: the challenge is on purpose, the bug is not. Contradictory data counts as unintended too, even when it sits inside a judgment task. An intentional challenge that fails is tagged with how it fails:

  • Learning · given away the answer is shown, circled or scripted before the learner thinks
  • Learning · too thin there is no real decision, or it can be guessed
  • Learning · unfair the facts are off screen or the language is above level
  • Learning · unsupported a wrong try gets only a vague correction

3 · What counts as a Clean Pass

A sitting is a Clean Pass only when all of these are true:

  1. 0 broken · 0 blocking · 0 unintended UI/UX friction, under the unified standard above.
  2. It was checked under replay conditions: played continuously from the segment’s start with no further Studio jumps, in English and Spanish, at 100% and 150%, by keyboard, with one plausible wrong try, Help opened and closed, and one reload mid-task.
  3. Its learning challenge is fair, or it has none. There is nothing given away, unfair or unsupported. Too thin is allowed only with a recorded design decision in the tracker.
  4. Every claim the app makes about the sitting matches what it actually checked (see the claims table).

Polish is logged but does not fail a sitting: spacing or alignment that costs no time and no understanding. Anything wrong in a number, name, date, instruction or button label is never polish.

Targets for the whole re-run:

  • All 42 sittings, plus the chooser, Stop here and the four endings, reach a Clean Pass.

  • A sitting that fails gets a fix-up, then its segment is replayed from the start, not just the one task.
  • Two tallies are reported side by side: UI/UX (clean / friction / block / broken) and learning challenge (fair / given away / too thin / unfair / unsupported / none).

Act I and parts of Act II and VI practice things a new worker really does on a Chromebook. The weakest transfer is where a button press stands in for the real act: sending a link without a link, matching receipts without reading them, an interview without speaking, or a privacy check that does not check privacy.

  • Prepares for: confirming a shift, reading a posted schedule against your own week, asking for a swap, attaching the final file (not the draft), clocking in and reporting a wrong punch, calling out before a shift, reading net and gross pay.
  • Matches: Gmail, and HotSchedules/ADP/When I Work-style portals.

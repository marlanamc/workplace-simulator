Turn 1 · Three homes for “what do I do right now”

One voice, one place, one sentence

Each option collapses today’s six instruction surfaces into a single guidance object that survives the desktop → job → done loop. Same Day One job in all three (1a, 1b, 1c) so you can compare like for like. Guidance fades by level in all three — Act I shows the sentence and the Show me; Act II shows the goal only; Act III shows a title bar.

1a

The Job Card — one card, always the same corner

One floating card, bottom-left, pinned above the shelf. It never moves, never closes, and is the only thing in the product that tells you what to do. It is on the desktop, it stays on top of the browser window, and it turns green when the job is done. Learners have to find one thing, once.

1b

Maria says — one human voice, spoken by default

The manager is the interface. Maria sits bottom-right and says one sentence at a time, out loud (auto-plays; replay always available). No system voice, no “Right now · 2/4” labels — the fiction carries the instruction, so following directions is the workplace skill being practised.

1c

The Ticket — a paper job slip that ticks itself off

Guidance as an object from the job, not from the software: a printed ticket. It sits on the desk, you carry it into the app where it docks as one strip under the tab bar, and each line checks itself as you do it. Almost no instruction text — the learner reads three short lines once and then watches them turn green.

What all three fix

  • One instruction voice on screen at a time — the briefing, dispatch strip, Right Now bar, nudge toast and done-screen copy stop competing.
  • Guidance survives the window change. Today it lives on the desktop and vanishes the moment the app opens.
  • One next action, never three buttons. “Do it again” and “Back to desktop” move into a quiet secondary spot.
  • Instruction text capped at one short sentence; the second language, the audio, and the pointer carry the rest.

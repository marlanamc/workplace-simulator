## The three words (say these out loud)

These names get mixed together at work. They are not three mystery settings —
they are two real permissions, plus extra words people use for the same thing.

| What it says | What you can do | What you cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| **Viewer** / **View only** / **Read only** | Look. Print. Copy the text. | Change anyone else's file. |
| **Commenter** | Leave a comment or suggestion. | Change the words or numbers. |
| **Editor** / **Can edit** | Change the file. | — (and everyone else with the file may see your changes) |

**View only** and **read only** mean the same thing. Google usually says
"View only" at the top of Docs and Sheets. Coworkers often say "it's
read-only."

## Simulator practice (~8 min) — once built
1. Open **Drive** or the link in Maria's email and open **Weekly Status
   Template** in **Sheets**.
2. Notice the file is **View only** — try to type in a cell. It should not
   change. (That's the point. The template is Maria's master.)
3. Open **File → Make a copy**. Keep the copy in your own Drive. Name it
   `status-week-of-aug-24` (same kind of name as Level 3's schedule file).
4. Open *your* copy. Confirm you can type now.
5. Leave the original template alone — do not ask for edit access, and do
   not share the master as **Can edit**.

Wrong clicks that should nudge, not punish:
- Typing in the template → "This is Maria's template. Make a copy so you
  don't change the original."
- **Share** on the template with **Can edit** → "That would let people
  change the master. Make your own copy instead."
- **Download** instead of Make a copy → "Download saves a file to this
  computer. Make a copy keeps a Google Sheet you can still share."

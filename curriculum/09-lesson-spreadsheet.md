# Lesson 9: Read and Trust a Total

**Time:** 20 minutes total (10 + 10) · **Track:** 4 — Growing at Work
**Task key:** `spreadsheet` · **Status:** not built yet.
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 8

## We will learn...
- enter numbers into a shared spreadsheet, and notice when a total looks
  wrong instead of assuming it's right because a computer calculated it.

## Before class (teacher prep) — once built
- [ ] Simulator: a small "weekly tally" sheet (a few rows of shift tips or
      hours) in a new Sheets-style Browser tab. One cell's total is planted
      to be wrong (a formula pointing at the wrong row, or a stale number).
- [ ] Google Classroom: post a real (simple) Google Sheet with the same
      shape — a few numbers and a total — with one number changed so the
      total no longer matches, and a prompt: *"Does this total look right?
      How do you know?"*

## Part A: Simulator (~10 min) — once built
1. Open the **Browser** → the new **Sheets**-style tab.
2. Enter the numbers you're given into the right cells.
3. Look at the total the sheet calculates.
4. Add up the numbers yourself (or estimate) and check: does the total
   match?
5. If it doesn't, don't just accept it — flag it, the same way you flagged
   an hours mismatch in Lesson 3.

## Part B: Real Practice (~10 min)
1. Open the real Google Sheet from Google Classroom.
2. Check the total the same way.
3. If it's wrong, write one sentence saying what looks off and who you'd
   tell.

**Optional extra challenge:** find *which* input number is most likely
wrong, not just that the total doesn't add up.

## Confidence check-in
- "I know how to double-check a total instead of just trusting it." 🙂 😐 🙁
- "A computer being wrong about a number still surprises me / doesn't
  surprise me anymore." 🙂 😐 🙁

## Notes for next time
- The spreadsheet itself isn't the skill here — checking a number instead of
  trusting it on sight is. Don't let this turn into a "how to use Sheets"
  lesson; the mechanics should take a student two minutes, tops.

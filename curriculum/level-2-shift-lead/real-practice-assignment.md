# Level 2 Real-World Practice: Shift Lead Wrap-Up

**When to assign:** only after a student has finished every track in
Level 2 in the simulator — Growing at Work (Lessons 1–3) **and** Planning
What's Next (Lessons 4–7). Not after each individual lesson.

**Why bundled, not per-lesson:** same reason as Level 1 — one focused
real-world assignment at the end of the level beats seven small ones
trickling in, one context-switch per skill. This is also the point where
work life and school life sit side by side for the first time, since
Track 4 and Track 5 both live in Level 2 together.

## Before class (teacher prep)
- [ ] Google Classroom: post **one** assignment — "Level 2 Wrap-Up" — with
      all of the following attached together:
  - a short written meeting-invite scenario (Lesson 1)
  - *(once built)* a shared Google Drive folder for the sharing scenario
    (Lesson 2)
  - *(once built)* a real Google Sheet with a wrong-looking total
    (Lesson 3)
  - a real (or realistic mock) community college application page/PDF
    (Lesson 4)
  - a real-looking mock financial aid award letter PDF (Lesson 5)
  - a real short syllabus excerpt with an assignment and due date
    (Lesson 6)
  - the same style of source-evaluation search task, using a real library
    database or Google Scholar (Lesson 7)
- [ ] Have the real FAFSA/financial-aid office contact info on hand — this
      level's content can surface real needs, not just practice ones.
- [ ] One combined instruction sheet, not seven — use the student
      instructions below as-is or adapt them.

## Student instructions (post as-is, or adapt)
You just finished Level 2 in the simulator — you're doing shift-lead work
*and* thinking about what's next. Now do the same things for real, in one
sitting if you can (it's fine to split this across two days if it's a lot):
1. Write the reply you'd actually send to the meeting-invite scenario —
   accept, decline, or propose a new time — and say why.
2. *(once built)* Share the matching file from the shared Drive folder with
   your teacher's email, at the right access level.
3. *(once built)* Check the real Google Sheet's total the same way you
   practiced — if it's wrong, write one sentence saying what looks off.
4. Find the application deadline and the required-documents checklist on
   the real college application, and tell your teacher what's still
   missing for you personally, if anything.
5. Find the amount and the accept-by deadline on the mock award letter.
6. Find the due date on the real syllabus excerpt and submit a real short
   response in Google Classroom.
7. Search a real library database or Google Scholar for the same style of
   topic, find one source you'd actually use, and say why it felt credible.

Turn it all in together.

**Optional extra challenge:** on #1, suggest two alternative times instead
of one; on #5, calculate how many days are left before the accept-by
deadline; on #7, name one source you were tempted to pick at first and
explain what changed your mind.

## Teacher review
Same approach as Level 1 — skim for completion, not perfection, and give
one or two specific notes back rather than a grade. #4 and #5 in particular
may surface a genuinely real question a student has about applying to
school; treat that as a real conversation, not a curriculum item to check
off.

## Confidence check-in — end of Level 2
The app already asked a version of this at the end of Lesson 7 — no need
to repeat it, but it's worth reading the student's answers with them:
- "Out of everything since the promotion (calendar, files, spreadsheets,
  college, financial aid, coursework, research), the thing I feel most
  ready for is: ___________"
- "One thing I'd tell a friend starting this whole program is: ___________"

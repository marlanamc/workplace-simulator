# Lesson 0.5: Keep Your Login Safe

**Time:** ~10–15 minutes · **Track 0** (before Level 1) · **Not a simulator level**
**Task key:** none yet (`phishing` is a stub for a later Mail task)
**Status:** real Chromebook only. Skip if the student already uses a Google
account at work or school and can say why they would not click a “reset
your password” email from an unknown address.
**Prerequisite:** 0.1–0.3, or teacher confirms they can open Chrome and type

## We will learn...
- keep a work login private (password, PIN, lock screen)
- pause on a message that asks for a password, a PIN, or a click “right now”

This is the first-week disaster that costs people jobs: locked out, shared
a PIN, or clicked a fake hours / IT email.

## Before class (teacher prep)
- [ ] Real Chromebook: student signed into *their* Google account, not a
      shared demo.
- [ ] Print or project two short emails (or Classroom posts):
      1. A normal note from a supervisor (name they know, no link asking
         for a password).
      2. A fake “IT / payroll” note: urgent, odd sender, asks them to
         click and enter their password or PIN.
- [ ] Confirm the class rule: nobody shares a password or PIN with a
      classmate, a “helper” on the phone, or an email.

## Practice (~10–15 min) — real Chromebook
1. Lock the screen. Unlock with their own PIN or password. (If the
   Chromebook uses a class PIN, still say: at a real job, that PIN is
   yours only.)
2. Name one place they should **never** type a work password (a text, a
   pop-up, an email link they did not expect).
3. Look at the two sample emails. Circle which one is fake, and say *why*
   in one sentence (odd sender, rush, asks for a password, spelling that
   feels off).
4. Say what they would do instead: do not click; tell a supervisor or
   teacher; go to the site by opening the bookmark, not the email link.

Nothing here is a real scam. The fake email is a classroom prop.

## Confidence check-in
- "I would not type my password because an email told me to." 🙂 😐 🙁
- "If I got locked out at work, I would: ___________"

## Notes for the teacher
- Do not collect real passwords. Do not have students type them into a
  form you made.
- Students who already live on a phone may treat email as “probably
  fine.” The fake vs real pair is the whole lesson. Keep it short.
- A simulator Mail task (`phishing`) can come later. This lesson should
  not wait on that build. First-week safety happens on day one, on the
  real device.
- After this, a student is ready for Level 1 (`mail`) whether or not they
  did 0.4 (Classroom download).

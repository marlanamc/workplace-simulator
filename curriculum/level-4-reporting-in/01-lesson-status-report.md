# Lesson 1: Send a Status Report

**Time:** ~15-20 minutes (whole level) · **Level:** 4 — Reporting In ·
**Track:** 5 — Reporting
**Task key:** `status-report` · **Status:** not built yet.
**Prerequisite:** Level 3 complete

## We will learn...
- pull a real number off the spreadsheet you already touched, write it into
  your own `=SUM(...)` formula instead of trusting one that's already
  there, and send a short status email that cc's a second person.

## Before class (teacher prep) — once built
- [ ] Simulator: reopen the Hsheets tab from Level 3 with this week's
      numbers already entered from that lesson. A blank total cell is
      waiting — the student types the formula themselves this time, instead
      of just checking one that's already filled in.
- [ ] No Google Classroom post needed yet — this is optional, supplementary
      practice bundled into the Level 4 wrap-up assignment
      (`real-practice-assignment.md`), offered once this lesson (all of
      Level 4) is done. Never a gate — a student who skips it still moves
      on.

## Simulator practice (~15-20 min) — once built
1. Open **Hsheets** — the total cell from Level 3's sheet is blank now.
2. Click the blank cell and type `=SUM(` followed by the range of cells
   with this week's numbers, then close the parenthesis and press Enter.
3. Check the total looks right (same "does this match what I'd expect"
   habit from Level 3).
4. Open **Hmail**, start a new message to your lead with the total, and
   click **Cc** to add a second recipient (a co-lead who also needs to see
   it) — the first time the app asks for more than one recipient.
5. Send it.

## Confidence check-in
- "I could write a simple total formula myself, not just check one someone
  else made." 🙂 😐 🙁
- "I understand when to cc someone on a work email." 🙂 😐 🙁

## Notes for next time
- This is deliberately the first lesson where a student *authors* something
  in a spreadsheet rather than just reading and verifying it — keep the
  formula itself simple (one `SUM`, no nesting); the cc habit is the other
  half of the point, not a footnote.

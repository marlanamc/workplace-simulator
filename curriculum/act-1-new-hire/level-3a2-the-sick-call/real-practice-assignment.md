# Levels 3a–3a2 Real-World Practice: The Email Shape, For Real

**When to offer:** after a student has finished both `mail-etiquette`
(Level 3a) and `call-out-sick` (Level 3a2) in the simulator. This is
**optional and supplementary** — a student who skips it still moves
straight on to Act II. Offer it during office hours, or post it to Google
Classroom for anyone who wants the extra practice.

**Why bundled across two levels:** the two lessons are one skill applied
twice — low stakes, then real stakes — so the real-world practice mirrors
that instead of treating them as unrelated.

## Before class (teacher prep, only if offering this)
- [ ] Google Classroom (optional): post **one** assignment — "Write It For
      Real" — with a short, plausible scenario (a shift conflict, a
      question from a coworker, or something from the student's own job if
      they have one).

## Student instructions (post as-is, or adapt — optional)
You just wrote two short work emails in the simulator. Try one for real:
1. Pick a real, low-stakes message you actually need to send this week —
   or use the scenario your teacher posted.
2. Write it using the shape from Level 3a: subject states the ask, first
   line gives the purpose, then the detail the other person needs, short
   close.
3. Send it for real, or turn in the draft if it's not ready to send yet.

**Optional extra challenge:** write the same message two ways — one to a
coworker, one to a manager — and note out loud what changed.

## Teacher review
Skim for the shape, not perfection — the simulator already did the graded
practice. One or two specific notes back is plenty: "the subject line did
its job," "try leading with the ask next time."

## Notes for the teacher
- This is the highest-value real-world message an hourly worker sends
  (calling out sick, in writing, before a shift). Worth a few minutes of
  class discussion even for students who skip the written assignment.
- A student is ready for Act II as soon as `call-out-sick` shows complete
  in their My Job panel, whether or not they did this optional practice.

# Pilot round 1 — the first sitting

**Status:** ready to facilitate; no participant observations recorded.

**Question:** Can a learner understand the purpose, begin independently, recover when confused, and explain a useful decision afterward?

Start with the opening experience. Do not ask someone to finish the whole course, and do not teach the interface before seeing where they get stuck. This guide is for the facilitator; the simulator’s Job Card remains the learner’s source of instructions.

## Opening script

**English:** “We’re trying out the course, not testing you. Please use it as you would on your own. You can use Help, take a break, or stop. If something is confusing or annoying, please tell me. When it feels comfortable, tell me what you’re looking for or expecting.”

**Español:** “Estamos probando el curso, no evaluándote a ti. Úsalo como lo usarías por tu cuenta. Puedes usar Ayuda, tomar un descanso o parar. Si algo te confunde o te molesta, dímelo. Cuando te resulte cómodo, cuéntame qué estás buscando o qué esperas que pase.”

Do not require continuous talking while a beginner is reading or typing. Ask what happened afterward if talking would distract them.

## Session sequence

1. **Welcome and walkthrough.** Let the learner start. Note whether they can explain what the simulator is for, recognize the Job Card, and understand how to move it. Do they mistake the practice workplace for real employment?
2. **First email work.** Continue into reply and attachment tasks if the learner is comfortable. Watch whether they distinguish reading, replying, and attaching the requested file. Record whether a starter helps them express their own answer or is copied without understanding.
3. **Schedule comparison, if reached.** Watch where they look before selecting a shift. Record whether they compare the work schedule and personal calendar or try each choice in turn. Do not name the conflict for them.
4. **Leave and return.** At a comfortable stopping point after a confirmed save, close/minimize the app and return. Then reload. Ask the learner to continue from where they think they left off. Record confusion about completed versus remaining work.
5. **Fresh example and short debrief.** Use one relevant prompt below for a task they actually attempted. Stop before fatigue makes the session uninformative.

Do not manufacture a mistake during the initial attempt. If the learner never needs Help, test recovery afterward in a clearly separate pass: invite them to explore a wrong choice and use the Job Card to recover. Label this **prompted recovery**, not spontaneous Help use. Test keyboard-only operation in a separate pass; do not force an unfamiliar input method into the initial usability observation.

## How to help without hiding a problem

When they ask for help or appear stuck, first ask: “What are you trying to do?” / “¿Qué estás tratando de hacer?” Record their explanation.

If they need help, give it. Start with a neutral prompt such as “Where would you look for help?” / “¿Dónde buscarías ayuda?” Then, if needed, point to the Job Card or demonstrate the next action. Record exactly what you supplied. Do not leave someone frustrated simply to collect a longer failure.

Use these outcome labels:

- **Independent:** completed without facilitator help; learner-chosen in-app Help is allowed and recorded separately.
- **Prompted:** needed a neutral facilitator prompt or a pointer to Help.
- **Assisted:** needed an answer, demonstration, or facilitator action.
- **Not observed:** not reached, interrupted, or stopped. This is not a failure.

## Fresh examples — show only the prompt

These examples are outside the simulator. An oral explanation or pointing is enough; do not add a writing test to a task about comparing information. Accept an explanation in the learner’s preferred language.

### A. Schedule comparison

**English:** “Your Tuesday shift is 9 AM–3 PM. Your appointment is Tuesday, 1–2 PM, with no travel time in this example. Available replacement shifts are Tuesday 8 AM–2 PM or Tuesday 3–9 PM. Which would you request, and why?”

**Español:** “Tu turno del martes es de 9 a. m. a 3 p. m. Tu cita es el martes de 1 a 2 p. m.; en este ejemplo no hay tiempo de traslado. Puedes pedir un turno de 8 a. m. a 2 p. m. o de 3 a 9 p. m., ambos el martes. ¿Cuál pedirías y por qué?”

### B. Attachment choice

**English:** “Your supervisor asks for this week’s schedule. The files are ‘Schedule — current week.pdf,’ ‘Schedule — last week.pdf,’ and ‘Safety handbook.pdf.’ Which would you attach? What would you check before sending?”

**Español:** “Tu supervisora pide el horario de esta semana. Los archivos son ‘Horario — semana actual.pdf’, ‘Horario — semana pasada.pdf’ y ‘Manual de seguridad.pdf’. ¿Cuál adjuntarías? ¿Qué revisarías antes de enviarlo?”

### Facilitator-only interpretation

A: The 3–9 PM shift avoids the appointment; 8 AM–2 PM still overlaps it. Look for an explanation using both time ranges, not just selection of the later option.

B: The current-week schedule matches the request. Useful checks include the actual file’s dates/content, recipient, and attachment. The filenames make this a small transfer probe; success does not prove they can manage unfamiliar real-world files.

For either example, record the first choice and the reason separately. If they choose correctly but cannot explain why, mark the evidence as unclear. If a neutral reread helps, record that too. One answer is not a mastery score.

## Debrief

Ask without suggesting the answer:

- “What was useful?” / “¿Qué te resultó útil?”
- “Where did you feel unsure what to do?” / “¿En qué momento no sabías qué hacer?”
- “Was there a step you wanted to skip? Why?” / “¿Hubo un paso que querías saltarte? ¿Por qué?”
- “What would you use outside this course?” / “¿Qué usarías fuera de este curso?”

Avoid “Was it easy?” or “Did you like it?” as the only evidence. Record specific behavior and the learner’s words rather than interpreting silence as boredom.

## Decide what to change

A dead end, lost work, inaccessible essential control, or misleading success message warrants investigation after one occurrence. A request to remove beginner repetition needs closer interpretation: did it help the beginner while boring the confident user, or did both repeat an action without making a new decision?

Prioritize the problems that block independent completion. For each, write one concrete change, recheck the original issue, and then give a fresh example. Do not redesign the whole course from one person’s preference.

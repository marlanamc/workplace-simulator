# Lesson independence review · October 4, 2026

Scope: implementation and content review of the 16 published lesson entries, plus the changed-facts and home examples for the eight Everyday Digital Confidence skills. This is a review of scaffolding, not a learner trial or evidence of independent performance.

Students should supply the task's answer, use a source, and check the result. Visible source facts, bilingual text, read-aloud, optional help, and realistic application defaults are useful support. They should not be removed merely to make a task harder. Guided and independent remain choices, not language-level assignments.

## Changes made

- **Sign in with a text code:** blank, editable email and password in all three lesson scenarios. Validate the full fictional address; forgive casing and surrounding spaces. Incorrect attempts preserve both entries. The reference facts remain in the Job Card. Story retains its remembered-account screen.
- **Choose a verification text:** classroom lessons now allow opening any message without immediately grading that choice. Feedback occurs when a code is submitted. Fresh examples no longer label the older message as expired; learners compare message times. Independent classroom copy states the goal without naming the correct sender.
- **Spreadsheet fresh examples:** open at A1 instead of selecting the first answer cell, B2. Learners choose the cell they intend to edit. Values remain blank and swapped rows are rejected even when their total matches.

## Remaining limits to observe in the pilot

- Templates and restricted simulators still simplify real applications. Use the teacher-facilitated real-tool activities to check transfer.
- The older optional follow-up rounds include selected-response decisions. They are useful decision practice, but do not prove a learner can attach, type, or submit in a real tool. The confidence scenarios use those actual simulated controls.
- Completion remains a practice count. Observe how a learner starts, what help they choose, how they correct an error, and whether they can repeat the task with changed facts.
- Do not take away help or require a wrong attempt before allowing a learner to request it. Keep language support separate from computer support.

Automated checks cover email validation, wrong-attempt preservation, restart, both languages, changed facts, and narrow-screen layout. Learner observation, actual Chromebook zoom and real-tool transfer remain necessary before claims about readiness.

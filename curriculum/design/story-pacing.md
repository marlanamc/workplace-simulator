# Story pacing: working direction

Owner clarification, 28 September 2026: there are no required dates. The story may span months or years. This applies to Story mode; standalone lessons remain separate.

## Narrative sequence to preserve

| Transition | What must make sense | Pacing approach |
|---|---|---|
| New hire → routine work | The learner understands the workplace and has worked enough shifts to receive a paycheck. | Keep the opening closely connected; include ordinary shifts between selected scenes. |
| Routine work → shift lead | Maria has observed reliability and provides support for new responsibilities. | Allow experience to accumulate and explain why the learner is trusted with more work. |
| Shift lead → route choice | The learner has enough experience to make a meaningful career decision. | Present each route as a choice with a reason, not an automatic promotion. |
| Stay and lead | Scheduling, reporting, meetings and budget work have real consequences for the team. | Let tasks recur over realistic work cycles; a weekly report and a later review need not happen the same week. |
| College | Application, aid and coursework happen in that order with time for responses and enrollment. | Separate application and term start. Match deadlines and aid notices to that sequence. |
| Front desk | A change of workplace includes orientation, supervision and an explanation of the learner’s role. | Establish the transition before patient-facing work; do not imply clinical authority. |
| Office hiring → HQ | Application, résumé, interview preparation, interview, offer and paperwork precede the start date. | Allow hiring and notice periods; explain the interview that occurs between played scenes. |
| HQ → Team Lead | The learner has gained office experience and has a defined reason to coordinate others. | Add a meaningful interval and an explicit change of responsibility; the current six-day jump needs revision. |
| Ending | The learner reflects on work actually practiced. | Mention growth without claiming that a handful of tasks proves professional mastery. |

## Implementation constraints

Choose dates after the narrative sequence is clear. No particular month, year or interval is a product requirement. Time jumps should be brief, bilingual story context in chapter/arrival copy, not extra instruction banners. Learning-session counters should not imply consecutive calendar days.

When a scene moves, inspect its emails, calendar, source documents, filenames/quarters, payroll periods, deadlines, forms and expected answers together. Historical material can legitimately predate the scene. Preserve relative constraints and valid weekdays, not arbitrary existing literals. Check all alternate routes independently.

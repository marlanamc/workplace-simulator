# Lesson 1: Find the Broken Formula

**Time:** ~15-20 minutes · **Level:** 7 — Weekly Numbers · **Track:** 8 —
Weekly Numbers
**Task key:** `formula-check` · **Status:** not built yet.
**Prerequisite:** Level 6 complete

## We will learn...
- use `SUM` and `AVERAGE` to summarize a week of numbers, and spot when
  someone else's formula is pointing at the wrong thing — not just whether
  a plain number looks off, like Level 3/4, but whether the *formula
  itself* is built correctly.

## Before class (teacher prep) — once built
- [ ] Simulator: reopen the team schedule sheet from Level 6, now with a
      week of hours filled in per person. A `SUM` and an `AVERAGE` cell are
      already there, but the `SUM` formula's range is one row short (it's
      missing the last person on the list) — so the total looks plausible
      but is quietly wrong.
- [ ] No Google Classroom post needed yet — this is optional, supplementary
      practice bundled into the Level 7 wrap-up assignment
      (`real-practice-assignment.md`), offered once this lesson (all of

      on.

## Simulator practice (~15-20 min) — once built
1. Open **Hsheets** → the week's hours grid.
2. Click the `SUM` cell and look at what range it's actually adding up
   (not just what number it shows).
3. Compare that range against the actual list of names — is anyone missing?
4. Fix the range so it includes everyone, and check the total updates.
5. Do the same check on the `AVERAGE` cell next to it.
6. Email your lead the corrected total, noting what was wrong.

## Confidence check-in
- "I can tell the difference between a total that looks right and a
  formula that's actually built right." 🙂 😐 🙁
- "If I found a mistake like this in something my manager built, I would
  tell them by: ___________"

## Notes for next time
- This is a step up from Level 3/4's "does the number look right" —
  here the student has to open the formula itself and reason about *why*
  it's wrong, not just notice a mismatch. Keep the fix to one clearly
  wrong range; don't stack multiple errors in one sheet.
